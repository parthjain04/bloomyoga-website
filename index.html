Six practices, four countries, and a class built around the person in front of me.

I trained in India and have taught in Australia and Canada since: corporate groups, community classes, beginners who had never been on a mat, and students working around an injury. I'm now in Dubai, teaching privately, in hotels and in offices across the city.

Most weeks are a mix. A strong morning flow for one group, something slower and more supported for someone managing chronic pain or stress. I would rather adjust the class than ask you to adjust to it.

What I teach

Six practices, and I move between them depending on who is in the room.

New to yoga? Start with Hatha or Vinyasa. Coming back from an injury, or managing something ongoing? Start with a conversation instead.

Hatha

Held postures, steady breath. The most adaptable of the four, and the best place to start.

Ashtanga

Fast-paced and structured. A set sequence of poses linked to synchronised breathing, practised the same way each time.

Vinyasa

Movement linked to breath, sequenced fresh each class. Best for groups with mixed experience.

Pranayama

Breath work, taught on its own or woven into a class. The quickest route into the nervous system, and the part most people skip.

Meditation

Short, guided and practical. Built to survive a working week rather than a retreat.

Yoga therapy

One-to-one work for a specific condition or injury, built over a series of sessions. More below.

Yoga therapy

Yoga for chronic pain and stress

It starts with a conversation, not a sequence. What's going on, what your medical team has told you, and what you want to be able to do again. Then I build a practice you can hold onto, and adjust it as things change.

  • Chronic pain, worked at a pace that doesn't set you back
  • Stress and sleep, through breath work and slower sequencing
  • Fatigue, where the aim is energy management, not exertion
  • Mobility and posture, often alongside physiotherapy
  • Nutrition advice, built around how you eat rather than a diet plan
  • A short home practice you'll actually keep up

Yoga therapy supports medical care. It doesn't replace it. I work alongside your doctor or physiotherapist, never instead of them.
